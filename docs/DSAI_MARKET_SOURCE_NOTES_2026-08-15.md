# DSAI Market Source Notes — 2026-08-15

The World Bank indicator page for “Individuals using the Internet (% of population) — Bangladesh” reports Bangladesh’s most recent value as **53% in 2024**. The indicator is presented as a World Bank/ITU data series and should be cited as a digital-access signal, not as a count of Bengali AI buyers.

The official Census India catalog contains **C-16: Population by mother tongue, West Bengal — 2011**, providing a primary source for Indian Bengali-language population analysis. The catalog page was reachable, but its dynamic page did not expose the exact Bengali row value in the browser extraction; do not invent a number from the catalog title alone. Use the downloadable Census table or an official census release before quoting a speaker count.

Additional verified context:

- UN DESA’s page on the 2015 international migrant stock reports India had the largest diaspora in the world at 16 million people living outside India in 2015, and lists Bangladesh among countries with large diasporas. This is broad diaspora context, not a Bengali-specific customer count and not current 2026 data.
- World Bank’s September 2024 remittances brief states that 184 million people were migrants worldwide in 2023 and global remittances to home countries totaled about $656 billion in 2023. This supports the existence of cross-border family/economic workflows, not DSAI demand by itself.
- OECD’s January 2026 announcement reports more than one-third of individuals across the OECD used generative AI tools in 2025; 20.2% of firms reported using AI in 2025, up from 14.2% in 2024. OECD coverage is not a Bangladesh/India Bengali-market estimate, but it signals a rapidly normalizing category with uneven adoption.
- McKinsey’s 2025 Global Survey reports 88% of respondents’ organizations regularly used AI in at least one business function, while most remained in experimentation or piloting; 62% were at least experimenting with AI agents. This is a survey signal, not audited market size or DSAI-specific demand.

Localization and adjacent-workflow evidence:

- UNESCO’s 2025 Global Roadmap for Multilingualism in the Digital Era states that language technologies such as machine translation, speech recognition, and NLP should strengthen the digital presence of all languages, and identifies community involvement, policy, capacity building, research/industry, and public awareness as priority action areas. This supports a Bengali localization opportunity only when community participation, data governance, provenance, and evaluation are included.
- The World Bank’s “South Asia’s Digital Opportunity: Accelerating Growth, Transforming Lives” identifies digital infrastructure, public platforms, digital financial services, digital skills, trust, regional integration, and relevant content/applications as core opportunities and bottlenecks. It specifically describes gaps in digital literacy, affordability, and relevant content/applications as usage barriers. This supports DSAI adjacent workflows around information access, education/training, public-service navigation, and cross-border team operations, but does not prove DSAI product-market fit.
- Bangladesh’s government-linked Brain Lab/EBLICT public site describes active Bengali NLP, speech, accessibility, human-centered AI, datasets, public-service conversational systems, and applied writing tools. It also shows that DSAI would enter an existing Bengali-language technology ecosystem; differentiation must therefore come from workflow execution, reviewability, distribution, and customer outcomes rather than Bengali UI alone.
