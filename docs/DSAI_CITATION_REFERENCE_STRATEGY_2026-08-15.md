# DSAI Citation and Reference Strategy

## The honest answer

Google and AI systems will not cite DSAI because DSAI requests it, adds a special AI file, or publishes a large number of keyword pages. Google states that AI Overviews and AI Mode use the same foundational SEO principles as normal Search, and that pages must be indexed and eligible for normal Search; inclusion and serving are not guaranteed.[1] Google also says there are no additional technical requirements or special schema needed for AI features.[2]

The practical target is therefore not “make the algorithm think DSAI is important.” The target is to become the clearest, most useful and best-supported public source for a narrow set of questions that DSAI can answer from first-hand product evidence. When another site, search engine or AI system needs a source for those questions, a clear public page should be the natural reference.

## Why DSAI could become reference-worthy

| Reference signal | DSAI implementation or required proof |
|---|---|
| Clear entity | Consistent DSAI name, public contact, domain, Organization metadata, Learn route and Evidence route |
| Crawlability | Public textual content, internal links, robots controls and sitemap |
| Originality | Product-state ledger, test evidence, safety commitments, Bengali workflow methodology and first-party pilot findings |
| Reliability | Version/date stamps, visible limits, source links, human editorial review and correction process |
| Usefulness | Direct answers to API safety, deployment, UAA/Creative workflow, Super Agent status and text-to-video limits |
| Independent corroboration | Consented customer case studies, partner references, community/industry links and public evaluation results |
| Freshness | Review dates, changelog, Search Console/Bing monitoring and updates when capability changes |

The first five signals are now represented in the DSAI public site. Independent corroboration and first-party outcome evidence must be earned through real pilots; they must not be invented.

## What was implemented

The new `/evidence` page is a citation-ready public ledger. It distinguishes live foundations from admin foundations and not-live capabilities, explains the build/test/bound method, publishes safety commitments, and links to the Learn guide and official Google guidance. The homepage links to `/learn` and `/evidence`, and the sitemap contains both routes. This makes the site easier for people and crawlers to navigate, but it does not guarantee ranking or citations.

## What DSAI must publish next

The strongest future reference pages should be original and narrow. First, publish a Bengali workflow methodology that explains how a notice or report becomes a draft and a human-reviewed action pack, with a reproducible example. Second, publish an evaluation note containing real, consented pilot measurements such as time-to-first-draft, correction rate, review time, repeat use and failure categories. Third, publish a capability changelog that records what entered preview, what became live, and what remains provider-gated. Fourth, publish a privacy and retention page that says what is stored, for how long, who can access it and how deletion requests are handled. Fifth, publish case studies only after written consent and verifiable outcomes.

## How search engines and AI systems may discover the evidence

Production operators should verify the domain in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the sitemap, inspect `/`, `/learn` and `/evidence`, and resolve crawl or indexing errors. Google recommends crawlable pages, visible text, internal links, good page experience and structured data that matches visible content.[2] Google’s structured-data policy says markup does not guarantee a rich result and must not mislead, hide unsupported claims or misrepresent ownership.[3] Bing’s AI Performance tooling can expose cited URLs, grounding queries and citation trends across supported AI experiences, allowing DSAI to learn which pages are being referenced and where clarity is missing.[4]

## Authority-building without manipulation

The ethical acquisition strategy is to share useful first-party research with Bengali-language communities, professional associations, educational and migrant-support organizations, responsible language-technology groups, and pilot partners. A partner should link to a page because it is useful, not because DSAI pays for a ranking link or scripts a promotion. Do not buy links, exchange irrelevant links, generate fake reviews, create fake social accounts, mass-post AI summaries, impersonate experts, or ask an AI system to cite DSAI without evidence.

The operator should maintain a monthly evidence register. Each row contains a claim, public URL, implementation version, source/evidence, reviewer, review date and next update. When a feature changes, update the page, sitemap timestamp where appropriate, structured data, changelog and Search Console inspection. If a claim cannot be verified, mark it as roadmap or remove it.

## 90-day reference-building plan

| Period | Action | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–14 | Verify domain, submit sitemap, inspect three public routes and establish a claim register | Indexed URLs, zero critical crawl errors, documented claim owners |
| Days 15–30 | Publish one Bengali methodology guide, one privacy/safety guide and one capability changelog | Three reviewed pages with sources and dates |
| Days 31–60 | Run 3–5 supervised pilots and measure time, corrections, review burden and repeat usage | Consent records, anonymized measurements and one approved case study |
| Days 61–90 | Share the evidence with relevant communities and partners; review Bing citation data where available | Qualified referrals, independent links, cited URLs and conversion events |

The success threshold is not traffic alone. DSAI should continue scaling only when it can show that qualified users understand the product, complete a useful workflow, return, and produce a margin after support and human review. Search visibility is an outcome of usefulness and trust, not a substitute for them.
