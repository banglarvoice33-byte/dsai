# DSAI Final Brand Action Plan

**Prepared by Manus AI · August 2026**

## The final brand decision

DSAI should launch as a **Bengali-first human-gated AI Workdesk for turning difficult information into clear, reviewable next steps**. This is more defensible than presenting DSAI as a general-purpose AI, a guaranteed-income system, an autonomous agent marketplace, or a text-to-video competitor before those capabilities are live and independently verified.

The brand should own one sentence:

> **জটিল তথ্য থেকে দায়িত্বশীল কাজের পরবর্তী ধাপ।**

The product experience should repeat three proof points at every important decision: **বাংলা-first clarity**, **human control when risk matters**, and **honest capability boundaries**. The final homepage CTA now expresses this controlled-alpha launch posture and presents these three principles as verifiable brand proof rather than decorative marketing.

## What a brand needs now

| Priority | DSAI action | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Keep one primary wedge: Bengali-first clarity for real-world documents, forms, notices, reports, and creative briefs | Homepage, Learn, Method, and Developer pages use the same promise |
| Trust | Show what is live, preview, roadmap, and provider-gated | Public evidence ledger, FAQ, privacy page, and connector console stay aligned |
| Conversion | Give users two unambiguous paths: join launch updates or sign in to work | Waitlist is labeled as a waitlist; sign-in starts the canonical OAuth route |

| Retention | Help a user complete one small useful workflow before introducing advanced capability | UAA draft and Creative brief queue remain the first repeatable product loop |
| Reliability | Measure field performance and errors by release and device class | RUM dashboard, release checklist, and rollback-ready checkpoints |
| Sustainability | Ship small, test-backed increments with explicit provider/legal gates | Monthly release train and capability ledger |

## The 30-60-90 day operating sequence

### Days 0–30: earn trust before expanding scope

Activate the final HTTPS domain, verify OAuth in a connected browser, confirm database backup and restore procedure, rotate any previously exposed secrets, and complete real-device checks at 320px, 390px, large text, low bandwidth, and offline reopen. Publish a short launch note that describes the controlled-alpha status, available workflows, privacy boundary, and contact channel. Do not publish fabricated customer counts, testimonials, social links, or “world’s fastest” claims.

The primary success measure is not traffic. It is the percentage of new visitors who understand the product and can complete one safe first action without confusion.

### Days 31–60: build a proof loop

Publish one high-quality Bengali/English answer each week for a real problem DSAI serves, link it to a public workflow, and record only aggregate, privacy-minimized outcomes. Use search analytics to discover unanswered questions, but do not publish raw personal queries. Invite a small, consented pilot group to submit representative UAA or Creative briefs. Turn verified patterns into case notes that describe the task, the draft improvement, the human review boundary, and what remains unavailable.

The primary success measure is repeat useful use: completed first workflow, return within 14 or 30 days, and successful resolution without misleading automation.

### Days 61–90: scale only what has evidence

Add one provider-gated integration only when its consent, rate limit, idempotency, audit, retention, failure, and rollback behavior are documented and staged. Add billing only after unit economics and refund handling are defined. Add Android packaging only after final-domain OAuth, Digital Asset Links, Data Safety declarations, and internal-track tests are complete. Keep the general Learn page for users and the Developer area for implementation details.

The primary success measure is contribution margin per completed workflow after provider cost, support time, and human review—not raw signups.

## Brand governance rule

Every new feature request should pass five questions before it becomes public copy:

1. Is the capability implemented in the current release?
2. Can a user complete it on the current domain with the current credentials?
3. Is its failure behavior safe and understandable?
4. Are consent, privacy, quota, retention, and human-review boundaries defined?
5. Is there a test, log, or public evidence page that supports the claim?

If any answer is no, the feature belongs in **preview**, **roadmap**, or **provider-gated** language. This rule protects the brand more effectively than adding more visual polish.

## Launch-day message architecture

The homepage should lead with the user’s problem, not the architecture. The first screen should communicate that DSAI helps people understand difficult information and move to a responsible next step in Bengali, Banglish, or English. The next screen should show the two real pathways. The trust section should explain the human gate. The FAQ should answer capability limits. The final CTA should offer a waitlist and a real sign-in route without conflating them.

The Developer page should contain API safety, deployment, OAuth, connector readiness, and operator routes. This separation keeps the brand approachable while preserving a serious technical surface for implementers.

## Measurement and update loop

Google’s current Web Vitals guidance recommends combining field data with lab data and evaluating the proportion of visits that meet good thresholds at the 75th percentile.[1] DSAI should therefore review LCP, INP, CLS, route errors, offline failures, OAuth failures, and form completion by release. WCAG 2.2 provides the accessibility baseline for web content across devices, while current mobile guidance highlights reflow, touch target sizing, pointer alternatives, and reduced redundant entry.[2] [3]

A release is ready when the code, content, tests, preview, environment validator, health/readiness checks, privacy boundaries, rollback path, and operator documentation agree. A brand claim is ready when the release evidence supports it. This creates a durable loop for adding new inventions without allowing the public promise to outrun the product.

## Final recommendation

The highest-value final action for DSAI is **not another broad feature expansion**. It is to launch one narrow, trustworthy workflow, measure whether people can complete it, publish the evidence, and use that evidence to choose the next capability. A focused product that repeatedly helps users is more valuable than a large platform that describes many future services.
